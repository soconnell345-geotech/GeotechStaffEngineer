# Candidate public references for geotech-references (checked 2026-11-10)

## How this was checked

- **"Wayback 200" / "archive capture 200"** in a USACE row means the Internet Archive holds an HTTP 200 copy of that official PDF made on the date given. My scripts could still read web.archive.org even though the fetch tool cannot. This is supporting evidence only, not confirmation on an official page.
- **"Resolved"** means I fetched the first 4 KB of the URL on 2026-11-10 and got HTTP 200/206 with a body that starts `%PDF` (or `PK` for the one spreadsheet). I give the file size the server reported. I did not download whole documents. For a few documents I read the first 0.3 to 1.5 MB to confirm a title page or a contents item; I say where.
- **DoD UFC/UFS status** comes from the WBDG public API (`https://www.wbdg.org/api/search?q=...` and `/api/documents/<id>`). It returns each record's status (ACTIVE / INACTIVE / ARCHIVED), publish date and file list. WBDG defines **INACTIVE** as "not regularly maintained due to limited use; may be used for new projects, but users must verify relevancy". It defines **ARCHIVED** as "outdated or superseded; prohibited for use in new projects".
- **USACE status: unverified, official site blocked.** publications.usace.army.mil returns 403 to every automated fetch, and I did not try to get around that. WBDG carries USACE ECBs but not EMs or ETLs, and armypubs does not carry USACE EMs. Every USACE row below is therefore marked **"unverified: official site blocked"** and gives the exact official URL to open in a browser (also collected in the last section). The supporting evidence for each row is second-hand: the official EM and ETL listing pages as archived by the Wayback Machine (EM page 1: 7 Oct 2026; page 2: 4 Sep 2024; page 3: 9 Jun 2025; ETL page: 18 Jul 2026), a recent HTTP 200 archive capture of each PDF, and web-search results.
- Nothing personal or project-specific went into any request.

## Priority-1 candidates in detail

**1. UFC 3-220-01 Geotechnical Engineering (1 Jul 2026).** This is the DoD criteria document: the requirements. The UFS 3-220-10 and 3-220-20 the library already holds are guidance. According to the WBDG change summary (15 Apr 2026), the new edition is a 24-page rewrite with commentary paragraphs. It keeps three requirement tables: minimum factor of safety for liquefaction (now including Risk Category V), and minimum factors of safety for foundations in rock and in soil. Those numbers are what the app should check its bearing, slope, pile and liquefaction results against for DoD-criteria projects. The 2026 edition is published only in WBDG's digital viewer, which I could not read with a plain fetch. The newest PDF is the 2012 edition with Change 3 (resolved). The WBDG record also lists UFC 3-220-07, 3-220-01A, 3-220-01N and 3-220-03FA as folded into this document. Public domain. **Action:** get the 2026 text by hand from the viewer, or wait for a PDF.

**2. EM 1110-2-1902 Slope Stability (31 Oct 2003).** Currency is **unverified: official site blocked**. It is on the archived USACE EM listing, an archive captured its PDF live on 21 Oct 2026, two third-party catalogues call the 2003 edition current, and web search found no revision. The library has no free dedicated slope manual: FHWA NHI-05-123 has no free full text (see rejected list). This EM gives the limit-equilibrium methods the app implements, USACE minimum factors of safety by load case, and guidance on drained, undrained and rapid-drawdown strengths. It supports the app's LE and SRM slope work. It does not conflict with UFS 3-220-10/20, which give little procedural detail on slopes. Public domain.

**3. EM 1110-2-1901 Seepage Analysis and Control for Dams (1986, Change 1 1993).** This is the USACE seepage manual: flow nets, seepage through foundations and embankments, control by cutoffs, blankets, drains and relief wells, and filter design (Appendix D, replaced in 1993). It supports the app's seepage module beyond the seepage chapter of UFS 3-220-10. Its filter criteria are older than NRCS NEH 633-26 (2017, #28), so cite NRCS for current filter rules. Public domain. On the archived EM listing; currency **unverified: official site blocked**.

**4. FHWA GEC-3, FHWA-NHI-11-032 (Aug 2011, Rev. 1).** Confirmed details: an NHI reference manual for course 130094, by E. Kavazanjian Jr. et al. It replaced the May 1997 GEC-3 and is still the edition on FHWA's geohazards page. The official PDF resolves at fhwa.dot.gov (20.4 MB), with an identical copy on ROSA P. According to FHWA and catalogue descriptions, it covers seismic hazard, site characterization, site response, liquefaction potential, liquefaction-induced ground displacement, seismic settlement, seismic slope stability and deformation, retaining walls, and foundations. I have not opened the settlement chapter today. That it uses the Tokimatsu-Seed and Ishihara-Yoshimine settlement methods is my recollection, not something I verified. It is the obvious public source for post-liquefaction settlement. It predates the 2014 Idriss-Boulanger update (#6). Public domain.

**5. USBR DS-13 Chapter 13, Seismic Analysis and Design (May 2015).** This answers the question of whether any public federal document reproduces the triggering procedures. Reading the first 0.8 MB of the PDF, §13.6 "Evaluating Liquefaction Potential" presents the Seed-Idriss simplified method and the Kσ correction (Boulanger 2003; Boulanger and Idriss 2004), and cites Youd et al. (2001), Cetin et al. (2004) and Idriss and Boulanger (2008, 2010). It is written for embankment dams but the triggering material is general. I did not check later sections (residual strength, deformation). Public domain.

**6. Boulanger & Idriss (2014), UCD/CGM-14/01.** This is the source of the current Idriss-Boulanger CPT and SPT triggering relationships, which revise the 2008 EERI monograph. The PDF is free on the author's UC Davis faculty site. It is **not** a government work: copyright belongs to the authors / UC Regents. Treat it as free to read and cite, but not to redistribute. Ask before storing its text or figures in the public repo. It complements #4 and #5, which predate it.

**7. USBR/USACE Best Practices in Dam and Levee Safety Risk Analysis (Jul 2019).** The USACE reliability ETLs the brief names (547, 556, 561) are no longer on the USACE ETL listing; see the rejected list. This joint Reclamation-USACE set is the current public federal text on geotechnical probability. A-1 covers probability and statistics; A-7 covers probabilistic limit-state (reliability) analysis; D-8 covers seismic risk for embankments, including liquefaction likelihood. It supports the app's FOSM, Monte Carlo and FORM work. Two caveats. First, USBR says the chapters are training material and "should not be used as a stand-alone reference". Second, I did not confirm that they print a coefficient-of-variation table. Public domain.

**8. NOAA "Air-Freezing Index Return Periods and Associated Probabilities".** This fills the NOAA frost-depth slot. It is an NCDC station table of air-freezing index (°F-days, base 32 °F) at return periods from 1.1 to 100 years, by state and station. NOAA's AFI page says to use the 100-year value for frost-protected shallow foundations. It covers US stations only, and I did not check the data period. For overseas sites, pair it with #9 and local climate records. Public domain.

**9. UFC 3-130-03 (Jan 2025 PDF).** This UFC absorbed UFC 3-130-06 (frost-depth calculation) and 3-130-04. From the first 1.5 MB: §2-4.2 covers seasonal frost when the freezing index exceeds 4,500 °F-days (with IBC and ASCE 32 pointers), and §6-3.1 "Depth of Freeze and Thaw" calls for the modified Berggren equation with n-factors and soil thermal properties. Table 9-1 gives adfreeze values. The 2026 digital edition, ACTIVE since 30 Sep 2026, cut it from about 46,200 to 8,100 words and dropped most tables and figures, per WBDG's change summary. **Hold the 2025 PDF** for content. Public domain.

**10. UFC 3-220-05 Dewatering and Groundwater Control (16 Jan 2004).** This is the planned slot, and its details are confirmed. WBDG lists it as INACTIVE: allowed on new projects, but users must check that the content is still relevant. It is not archived. The PDF resolves (3.5 MB). It covers well and wellpoint design, pressure relief, aquifer characterization and cutoffs. Newer federal companions: ETL 1110-2-586 (2023, expired 30 Jun 2026, #22), USBR DS-13 Ch. 21 (2014, #23) and the USBR Ground Water Manual (#27). Public domain.

**11. EPRI EL-6800 (Aug 1990).** This is the classic soil-property correlation manual prepared by Cornell University. Naming Kulhawy and Mayne as authors is my recollection; the EPRI record names Cornell as contractor. It covers correlations from SPT, CPT, DMT, PMT and index tests to strength, stiffness, stress history and permeability, with variability information. It supports the app's correlations and reliability inputs. EPRI's public-download endpoint serves the PDF free. It is **copyrighted by EPRI**: free to read and cite, not to redistribute.

**12. UFC 3-301-01 OCONUS Ground Motions spreadsheet (10 Feb 2025).** This is the DoD table of seismic design ground motions for overseas locations, attached as a related file to the active UFC 3-301-01. For overseas building sites it is the governing seismic input, ahead of any site-response step. I did not open the spreadsheet, so I have not checked whether it carries multi-period (ASCE 7-22) parameters. I also did not check whether the library's held UFC 3-301-01 (2023 c4) still prints these values. Public domain. The same record has OCONUS snow and wind spreadsheets.
