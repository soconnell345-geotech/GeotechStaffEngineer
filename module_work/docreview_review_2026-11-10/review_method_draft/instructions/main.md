## Reviewing a drawing set or a specification

This adds a review method to your reading rules. Use it when the user asks for a review, a check or a comparison of a set; not for a single question.

### Before the sheets

1. Establish what is being reviewed and against what. From the document: the project, the disciplines, the issue (60 %, issued for construction, a revision and its date). From the user: their role, and what the set is checked against (the specification, an earlier revision, a standard they supply, their own checklist). If they have not said, state what you assumed in your first line and go on; ask only when the answer changes which pages you read.
2. List the sheets: number, title, revision and date from each title block, compared with the sheet index if there is one. A sheet in the index and not in the file, or the reverse, or a title or revision that differs, is a finding before any sheet is read.
3. Plan the reading by sheet in `write_todos`, and say at the start how much of the set this turn can cover.

### On each sheet

Read notes and schedules from text where the text layer has them, then look. Check what the sheet says about itself:
- the scale stated for each view; north arrow and grid on plans; units;
- every callout, section mark and detail mark: note the sheet and detail it points to;
- dimension strings: the parts add up to the overall where both are given;
- tags against the schedule or legend: a tag with no row, a row with no tag;
- notes that defer or leave open ("see specification", "by others", "TBD", "verify in field"), "typical" where the condition shown is not, and anything clouded or on hold.

### Across sheets

A sheet-by-sheet reading misses these. Work from your notes, then look at both places before you report:
- each callout resolves: the sheet exists, carries that detail or section, and shows the same thing;
- one element on two sheets agrees: grid lines and spacing; levels between plan, section and elevation; sizes and tags between plan and schedule; the same area between disciplines;
- general notes against details: a cover, a lap, a grade or a tolerance given twice with two values;
- drawing notes against a specification the user supplied: a section cited that it does not have; a product or grade that differs;
- a revision: what the cloud and the revision table say changed, and whether the sheets that depend on it changed too.

### What a comment rests on

State the basis of every comment. There are three:
1. the document itself: two places that disagree, or something it calls for and does not show;
2. a document the user supplied, cited the same way;
3. general practice, said as such: "common practice is ...; confirm against the governing code or the owner's standard".

Never give a code or standard clause number from memory, and never say something violates a code you were not given. Where a check needs a calculation or the designer's intent, write a question.

### The comment log

One row per comment, in the order of the set: number; sheet and detail, or specification section; location (grid, view, note number); what the document shows (quote or describe); the comment or question; its basis; its kind (conflict, missing information, unclear, coordination between disciplines, constructability, drafting); how checked (read from text, seen, seen at zoom).

Close with what you covered: sheets read, sheets looked at, sheets not reached, and the checks you could not make and why.
